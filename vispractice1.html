Chapter 3

Where Does the Story Come From?

Here's a question that rarely gets asked out loud: does it matter where an anime's story originally came from? Before the animation studio touches it, before the voice actors are cast, before the soundtrack is composed — the story has a life somewhere else.

Some anime are adapted from manga that ran for years in Weekly Shōnen Jump, building up millions of dedicated readers who will show up on day one to rate and review the anime. Some come from light novels — serialized prose fiction with passionate online followings. Some are based on video games, visual novels, or card games. And some are entirely original — no source material, no preexisting fanbase, just an idea a studio had from scratch.

The difference in average ratings between these categories turns out to be one of the strongest signals in our entire dataset. It's not subtle. The chart tells a clear story.

The Quality Question

More anime inevitably means more filler, but surprisingly, average scores have held steady or even improved in recent years. Early anime from the 60s and 70s scores lower, possibly due to fewer ratings and different storytelling norms.

05

Source Material Matters

Manga and light novel adaptations dominate the top of the ratings chart. These stories arrived pre-tested — fans already loved them before a single frame was animated. The anime adaptation becomes a reward for an existing audience, and that loyalty shows up in the scores.

Original anime — created without any source material — faces a much harder road. No built-in fans, no proof of concept, no head start.
